## 3 · Q1: why could JLR borrow at less than half the 2011 rate?
43. Divider · 03 Q1
44. **Q1 asks what let JLR's coupon fall from 8.125% to 3.5%** · question; three candidate causes: Treasury rates, maturity and features, JLR's own credit; assumptions (issued at par, tenor-matched Treasuries)
45. **A corporate bond's yield = a Treasury yield + a credit spread** · CFA FI LM07, two-layer picture
46. **2011: 8.125% = 3.15% (10-year Treasury) + 497bp** · calc
47. **2015: 3.50% = 1.57% (5-year Treasury) + 193bp** · calc
48. **Two thirds of the 462bp fall came from a narrower credit spread** · waterfall: credit 304bp (66%), Treasury level and shorter maturity 158bp (34%)
49. **Like for like at 5 years, Treasuries fell only 26bp** · 1.83% → 1.57%
50. **Test 1, the same bond: the 2011 notes' own yield fell from 8.125% at issue to 2.28% (to the call) in 2015** · same issuer, same bond (Exhibit 2)
51. **Test 2, every issue: JLR's spread over Treasuries fell issue by issue** · spread at issue for each US$ note 2011–2015 (from slide 23) [src]
52. **Test 3, the market: high-yield spreads overall fell only about 40bp; JLR's fell 304bp** · US high-yield index ~502bp (May 2011, BofA Merrill Lynch via Bloomberg) vs ~460bp (Mar 2015, Fortune) [src; secondary sources, dates approximate] → about 260bp of the fall is JLR-specific
53. **Why JLR's spread narrowed: a far safer borrower** · dashboard from Exhibits 4–6 (EBITDA margin, net cash, D/E, cover) + rating path
54. **The 2015 notes are also shorter: 5 years instead of 10, with no call period to pay for** · tenor effect; the 2011 notes were sold as callable after five years, an option investors charged for [src]; the 2020 notes' redemption terms were not found, so no claim is made about them
55. **Q1 answer: mostly JLR's better credit, a little lower rates and a shorter maturity** · summary with the waterfall

## 4 · Q2: what price would bondholders accept?
56. Divider · 04 Q2
57. **Q2 asks what price would make holders give up their 8.125% notes** · question; holder's choices: sell in the market, keep (and be called in 2016), tender early or late, roll into the new notes
58. **A bond's price has two parts: the clean price and accrued interest** · CFA definitions; the tender paid accrued separately
59. **Accrued interest on 17 March 2015: 122 days → US$27.53 per US$1,000** · 30/360 step calc; matches the payment JLR made [src]; 31 March: 136 days → US$30.69
60. **The cash flows still to come: US$40.625 every May and November, then US$1,000** · timeline to 2021
61. **Method A, the naive view: discount to 2021 at JLR's new 3.5% rate → 125.43** · why 3.5% (JLR's current cost of debt), the 13 cash flows discounted (table over 2 slides; repeats get their own slides, user decision)
62. **Method A, continued: full price 128.18 − accrued 2.75 = clean 125.43, a 25% premium** · result
63. **No holder will get 125: JLR can repay the notes in May 2016** · what a call option is; call schedule: before May 2016 make-whole, from 15 May 2016 at 104.0625% [src]
64. **With an 8.125% coupon and a 3.5% market rate, JLR is certain to call: the bond behaves like a 14-month bond** · yield to call vs yield to maturity; yield-to-worst
65. **A callable bond is worth the straight bond minus the issuer's option: 125.43 − 111.28 ≈ 14 points** · CFA: callable = straight − call option; negative convexity (price capped near the call price)
66. **Method B: to the May 2016 call at 3.5% → 109.11** · 3 coupons + 104.0625, step table
67. **For 14 months, the right discount rate is far below 3.5%** · whose rate: a holder's required yield for 14-month JLR risk; 1-year Treasury 0.26%; sensitivity: value to call 112.85 at 0.50% → 109.11 at 3.50%
68. **The market agreed: 111.28 implies 2.28% to the call (price from 4 Feb 2015)** · Exhibit 2 check
69. **The actual tender used this logic: priced to the May 2016 call at the 0.25% May 2016 Treasury (0.395%) + 0.50%** · tender formula [src: JLR pricing release]
70. **Step by step: discounting at 0.895% gives 112.35** · step table: 4.0625, 4.0625, 108.125 discounted → full 115.10 − accrued 2.75 = 112.35
71. **Early tenders got US$1,123.52 per US$1,000, late ones US$1,093.52, both plus accrued interest** · early premium US$30 [src]
72. **Four answers, one picture: 109.11, 111.28, 112.35 and 125.43** · reconciliation bar chart, each labelled with method and discount rate
73. **Valuing the notes as if they ran to 2021 overstates them by about 13 points** · paying 125 would hand holders ~US$54m for an option JLR owns
74. **JLR's filings give 15 November 2021 as the maturity; using it moves only the naive value (127.26 vs 125.43), not our answer** · case date used throughout (user decision); filing quoted [src]
75. **Q2 answer: holders would accept about 111–112 clean per 100 (US$456–461m for the issue) plus accrued interest** · summary

## 5 · Q3: incremental cash flows and savings
76. Divider · 05 Q3
77. **Q3 asks whether buying back all the notes at the Q2 price saves JLR money** · split: accounting saving vs economic value; pre-tax vs after-tax
78. **Assumptions** · all US$410m bought at 112.35 (the actual early price, the top of our Q2 range; the low end is in the sensitivity) on 17 Mar 2015; funded with new 3.5% debt; UK tax 20% [src]; US$; fees ignored, then noted
79. **JLR needs US$471.9m to buy the notes, so the US$500m issue leaves only US$28.1m before fees** · 410 × (112.35 + 2.75)%
80. **The accounting view: interest falls by US$16.8m a year on like-for-like funding** · old US$33.3m (8.125% × 410) vs new US$16.5m (3.5% × 471.9)
81. **But JLR pays US$50.6m above face up front, three years of savings** · premium incl. US$12.3m early premium
82. **Incremental cash flows, 2015–2017** · table: old coupons avoided, new coupons paid, net (US$m)
83. **Incremental cash flows, 2018–2019**
84. **Incremental cash flows, 2020–2021** · new debt repaid Mar 2020; old principal avoided May 2021
85. **Against holding the notes to 2021, the NPV looks positive: +US$53.5m** · NPV at 3.5% (JLR's pre-tax cost of new debt) step summary
86. **But JLR would have called them in May 2016 and refinanced: against that, NPV is −US$13.4m** · the realistic baseline; after the call JLR refinances at its market rate, which has zero NPV, so the horizons line up to 2021
87. **After tax the picture is the same: +US$44.8m vs hold, −US$10.4m vs call** · after-tax flows at 20%, premium deductible, discount at 3.5% × (1 − 0.20) = 2.80%
88. **At market value the deal is about break-even: −US$4.4m** · (111.28 − 112.35) × US$4.10m; caveat that the price is from 4 Feb
89. **The NPV depends on what you compare it with** · comparison table: hold / call / market, pre- and after-tax, and sensitivity to the discount rate
90. **Q3 answer: a real cash-interest saving, but no economic saving; JLR paid the gain away up front** · summary; efficient market
