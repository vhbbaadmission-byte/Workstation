### A: lead-agent check (my own calculation, not a quote)
- The US$1,123.52 total consideration reproduces to the cent as: price to the FIRST CALL date (15 May 2016) at the call price 104.0625% (US$1,040.625 / 1,040.63 per US$1,000), discounted at 0.395% + 0.50% = 0.895% yield, semi-annual, 30/360, settlement 17 Mar 2015, less accrued US$27.53. I got US$1,123.51-1,123.52. So the tender price was a yield-to-first-call (not yield-to-maturity) price, which is why the reference security is the 0.25% UST due 15 May 2016 (matches the first call date). The US$30 early premium is already inside the total (late = total less 30).
- Using 104.663% instead (the figure printed in the 2015 offering memorandum, see B) gives US$1,129.46, which does not match. So 104.663% in that OM is almost certainly a typo for 104.063%.
- Accrued: 122 days (15 Nov 2014 to 17 Mar 2015, 30/360) x 81.25/360 = 27.53. 136 days to 31 Mar = 30.69. Coupons are 15 May / 15 Nov.
- Total consideration US$1,123.52 per US$1,000 = 112.352% of face (premium of 12.35% over par incl. the early premium; 109.352% late). Accrued is additional.

### B: flags for the lead
- Call price: OM says 104.663%; coupon/2 convention and the exact reproduction of the tender price (see A check) point to 104.0625% (written 104.063%). The 2012 notes in the same OM (8.250%) show 104.125% = 100 + 8.25/2, so the same pattern fits. Treat 104.663% as a typo; I did not find the indenture to settle it. Call price steps in later years (not found): typical high-yield schedule would be 104.063 (2016), 102.708 (2017), 101.354 (2018), 100 (2019+), but this is NOT sourced.
- Before 15 May 2016: "100% plus a redemption premium" is the standard make-whole (Treasury + 50 bp style). The exact make-whole formula for the 2011 notes was not found (the OM only gives it for the 3.875% 2023 notes).
- Issue price of the 2011 notes: not found.
